## Recruiting concentration and Board + Focus + Offer

Early play showed that a controlled Program could concentrate normalized
attention on a tiny elite group and sign nearly everyone, even at low Prestige.
Generated AI Boards spread attention broadly, producing a large controlled/AI
advantage unrelated to intended strategy.

The accepted resolution replaced Priority 1–5 and normalized attention with:

```text
Board baseline effort = 3
Focus bonus = +3
Maximum Focus targets = 3
```

Offers became position-capacity commitments and AI plans were aligned so valid
premium Focus pursuits normally carry Offers. Manual Focus and Offers remain
independent. Recruiting mechanics and Prestige were frozen after acceptance.

## Recruit Talent Distribution and POT finalization

The original Recruit population had too many ready-made strong freshmen and
too little distinction between readiness, National Rank, and Potential. Recruit
Talent Distribution V1 separated readiness from ceiling while preserving
`POT ≥ OVR`; National Rank uses both OVR and POT. This created ready-now,
developmental, and raw-upside prospects.

Later multi-Season play found compressed elite Recruit POT gaps. The accepted
Recruit POT Candidate B finalizer preserved a bounded development runway for
eligible high-OVR Recruits without materially disturbing class/rank/Team
economies. This accepted POT candidate is unrelated to the rejected historical
OVR Experiment B v1.

## Player Development V1

Manual examples showed high-POT raw prospects often developing almost exactly
like low-POT peers. Diagnostics confirmed the old model used Potential mainly
as a cap and structurally limited annual gains.

Development V1 added class baseline, POT-headroom opportunity, stable hidden
tendency, annual variance, rare breakouts, and a hard POT cap. Acceptance runs
produced materially different equal-OVR outcomes by POT while preserving busts,
modest careers, hits, and rare stars. Concentrated single-attribute gains remain
a live WATCH item in current Playtesting.

## Mature league and rebuild observations

Pine Valley established that a low-Prestige rebuild can remain a genuine
multi-year challenge. Around Season 8, one observed best Team was roughly 83
OVR while most Programs were in the 70s. This was interesting rather than an
automatic defect; low-prestige feel and long-run powerhouse ceilings remain
monitor-only without new evidence.

## Rotation V1 and workload realism

Natural-position-only Rotation editing created inflexibility and unrealistic
defaults. Rotation V1 introduced floor-position assignments with adjacent
secondary eligibility while preserving natural position as Player identity.
Behavioral diagnostics and manual play validated legal 200-minute allocations.

Later play found automatic 40-minute leaders too common. Accepted role-aware
defaults reserve 36 minutes for top-three OVR Players, cap ordinary Players
with backups at 32, and prevent automatic natural 36→40 secondary promotion.
Manual legal 40-minute assignments remain supported. Interior/forward-heavy
secondary paths and rare incumbent displacement remain WATCH.

## Tournament selection, seeding, and completion

Early seeding protected automatic qualifiers too strongly. The accepted model
keeps four automatic qualifiers plus 12 at-larges, then seeds all 16 through
the same results-only résumé comparator. Game Simulation did not require
retuning. Tournament upset variance remains an accepted non-issue absent new
evidence.

A reported navigation/progression escape path after Tournament completion was
not reproduced at the time; focused coverage established that League navigation
did not bypass Late Recruiting, finalization, or Offseason checkpoints. Later
normal play reproduced a distinct presentation-scoping failure through the
final regular-season review; current status and manual confirmation needs live
in active `PLAYTESTING.md`.

## Recruiting visibility and information architecture

Manual play repeatedly showed important commitment and battle context happening
without enough visibility. The accepted progression was:

- pure battle/readiness and commitment-activity projections;
- Hub status, Board management, Battles intelligence, National discovery;
- generated-plan Focus/Offer coherence;
- six accepted readiness states, replacing misleading `Early Interest`;
- explicit Fill Remaining Board;
- compact Recruiting overview and a canonical Guide destination; and
- quiet completed-class treatment when no openings remain.

Premium Offer allocation remains WATCH because observed scarcity often reflected
capacity allocation or other-target selection; it has not earned a production
change.

## Season/Postseason and Coaching presentation

Successive manual passes resolved oversized Hub spacing, moving controls after
Quick Sim, fragmented Recruiting recaps, redundant root League chrome, and weak
Tournament-completion hierarchy. The accepted Hub uses stable pregame/completed
card geometry, compact Game Leaders, controlled-Conference standings, and a
two-column competition/Recruiting composition.

Coaching became a permanent Dynasty destination using existing canonical
Rotation commit boundaries. Simple Rotation became the default editor while
Advanced retained exact positional control. Starting Five/Bench/Reserves is a
derived presentation over committed Rotation, not canonical starter state.

## Followed Players V1 acceptance

Manual cross-Program play validated the core retrieval loop: discover a Player,
open details, Follow, return through League, and retrieve the Player later.
Following intent is stable-ID user intent and has no basketball effect.

The original active-only projection later evolved in Phase 7B.2. Current truth
is `active | former | unknown`; see current Playtesting and Architecture.

## Phase 7B.1 — Around the Country / News acceptance

Manual play confirmed that partial rounds publish no stories, fully completed
rounds publish one grouped checkpoint, Player/Program links reuse existing
details, Back restores the originating League tab, and fresh League entry
returns to News. Mobile acceptance at 390px kept tabs and stories readable.

A multi-Season playthrough demonstrated the intended attachment loop. Avery
Martin was discovered through a 43-point story, Followed, recognized repeatedly
as a scoring leader, and remembered through a senior Tournament exit. Other
memorable signals included a 23-rebound/7-block game, an 18-assist freshman,
five-star commitments, win streaks, undefeated-run endings, and major upsets.

Post-acceptance polish added restrained multi-achievement copy, game-outcome
context, explicit Recruit-rank wording, top-seed upset context, and a quiet
latest-empty-checkpoint status without changing story eligibility.

## Phase 7B.2 — Player Legacy / Alumni acceptance

The Avery-style lifecycle loop was completed: a Followed senior remained in
the stable Follow list after archival, moved from Active to Former after
rollover, and opened Historical Player Details.

Acceptance verified final Program, position/height, observed Season range,
regular-season career aggregation, Final/Peak OVR, Final Ratings, chronological
Career Progression, optional canonical Recruiting Origin, Follow controls, and
Program/Back navigation. Original-Universe graduates correctly omit Recruiting
Origin; stale IDs remain unknown rather than being mislabeled Former.

Postseason career totals, historical Tournament game logs, Tournament résumé,
Awards, records, signature games, broad Alumni browsing, and global historical
Player search were deliberately excluded.

## Phase 7B.3 — Season Preview acceptance

Manual acceptance verified the full-width Round 1 Hub doorway, persistent
League News access while an active Season is retained, Season 1 Established
Players/Freshmen to Know, rollover returning/leap/freshman sections, and
Preview → Player/Program Details → Back navigation.

At 390px the page retained its viewport width and each compact table scrolled
inside its own container. Automated coverage established deterministic ordering,
Rounds 1–2 promotion timing, real rollover projection, exact prior archive and
Recruiting-class invariants, and no Preview persistence or simulation change.

## Player Identity gameplay signal

The manual concern was that some Seasons felt homogeneous and lacked obvious
national superstars, while other play produced memorable scoring, passing, and
defensive outliers. Characterization concluded that conventional identities
mostly translate, current role-aware minutes soften raw peaks, Steals show the
clearest compression, and rare cross-position identities face both profile-
supply and translation limits.

The product decision was to park tuning because the current population is good
enough for feature development. Canonical generation/OVR remain production
truth. Full quantitative characterization, Candidate A/A V2, rejected OVR
Experiment B v1, and reopening criteria live only in
`PLAYER_IDENTITY_RESEARCH.md`.
